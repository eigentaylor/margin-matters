About Census Reapportionment

Every 10 years, following the decennial census, the 435 seats in the U.S. House of Representatives are reapportioned among the states based on population changes. Since a state's electoral vote count equals its number of House seats plus its two senators, reapportionment directly affects presidential elections.

Key historical notes:

  • The total number of electoral votes has changed as new states were admitted and DC gained voting rights (1961)
  • The House was capped at 435 members in 1929, making reapportionment a zero-sum game
  • Population shifts from the Rust Belt to the Sun Belt have been the dominant trend since the 1970s
