Here we plot the change in raw Democratic votes (horizontal axis) versus the change in raw Republican votes (vertical axis) for each unit (state or district) between two elections. We have four quadrants:
  • The top-right quadrant (green points) represents units where both parties gained votes. This is common in high-turnout elections.
  • The bottom-left quadrant (purple points) represents units where both parties lost votes. This can happen in low-turnout elections or when third-party candidates gain traction.
  • The top-left quadrant (red points) represents units where Democratic votes decreased while Republican votes increased.
  • The bottom-right quadrant (blue points) represents units where Democratic votes increased while Republican votes decreased. This is often seen in blue wave elections.
  • Points near the origin (amber points) indicate minimal change in vote totals for both parties, suggesting stability or low turnout changes.
There are also four notable regions on the different sides of the diagonal lines. The downward sloping line separates overall turnout increases (above the line) from overall turnout decreases (below the line). The upward sloping line separates units where Democratic vote changes dominate (right of the line) from those where Republican vote changes dominate (left of the line). Thus:
  • Above the line sloping downwards, in the blue and green regions, we have units where overall turnout increased. In the blue region, Republican votes increased more than Democratic votes, and vice versa in the green region.
  • Below the line sloping downwards, in the purple and amber regions, we have units where overall turnout decreased. In the purple region, the loss was greater for the Democrats, and in the amber region, the loss was greater for the Republicans.
