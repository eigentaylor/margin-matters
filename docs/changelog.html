Changelog

A record of changes, updates, and improvements to this site.

August 27, 2026

Sim2028


  • Fixed the opening title card's win-probability tiles showing a literal "0.0%" for a trailing candidate - these are Monte Carlo estimates, not mathematical certainties, so a candidate that didn't happen to win any sampled runs now shows a small non-zero floor (matching the leading candidate's existing never-show-100% cap) instead of a false 0%. Also fixed the exported Election Night Album's version of this card, which additionally never subtracted the third-party candidate's probability from the Republican figure (overstating it) and never showed a third-party win-probability tile at all, unlike the live view.
  • Fixed election night mis-rendering an outright third-party win as a "no majority yet" screen when it happened mid-count rather than at the very end - the mid-count "clinches the presidency" checkpoint screen (with portrait and "PROJECTED TO BE Nth PRESIDENT" label) only ever fired for a D or R candidate, so a third-party candidate crossing 270 electoral votes before every state was called fell through to the no-majority screen instead, only getting corrected at the final tally.
  • The "Nth President" ordinal label (e.g. "48th President") now also appears for a third-party winner instead of falling back to the generic "Elected President" - the numbering was never party-specific to begin with, so a third-party candidate winning gets the same "next new president" number a D or R newcomer would.
  • The final-tally recap's "206 D – 56 R (276 Other)" line now reads "206 {D's last name} – 56 {R's last name} – 276 {O's last name}" instead of bare party letters, in both the live checkpoint popup and the exported album image, and the numbers now have a dark outline so they stay legible against any of the card's accent colors (including the gold background of a third-party win, where the plain colored text was hard to read). The third-party segment is now sized and colored the same as the other two instead of a smaller, muted aside.
  • Added a "National popular vote polling" box above the state polling table, showing the current poll's raw Democratic/Republican/Undecided shares (plus Third party when enabled) - previously that breakdown only existed in the debug panel.
August 26, 2026

Sim2028


  • Split the campaign map's polling tooltip so each candidate gets its own row instead of cramming the whole D/R/(third-party)/Undecided share breakdown and the derived margin onto one crowded "Poll" line. The derived margin now sits in its own row right below the 2020/2024 history rows, followed by a row per candidate (using their actual last names when a pick is active) and an Undecided row.
  • Fixed that same tooltip's "Poll" margin row to measure whoever's actually leading against the real runner-up instead of always reporting the D-vs-R gap - a state where a third-party candidate was genuinely the frontrunner or runner-up was showing a misleading margin (e.g. "D+9.9") instead of the real race, even though the rating badge right above it ("Lean O") already had it right. That row now labels the leader by their last name (e.g. "Vance+12.6", "Perot+8.2") rather than the D/R/O letter used elsewhere in the app.
  • Aleck Lickman now reacts a third time on election night, right after whichever comes first - the national popular vote getting called, or a candidate clinching the presidency (270+ electoral votes) - with dialogue that either claims credit or moves the goalposts depending on whether that call matched his prediction and how the still-uncalled race is currently trending. Appears in both the live simulation and the "Election Night Album" export.
  • Fixed election night so a sim2028 run with the third-party mechanic turned on always shows that candidate in the opening title-card portrait and the running electoral-vote tally, even on a run where they don't end up winning any states - previously both only appeared once the third party actually picked up an electoral vote, which made the setting invisible on most runs of it.
  • Fixed the third-party mechanic so a maxed-out "strength" + home-state bonus can no longer wipe a major party's vote share down to a literal (and unrealistic) 0% in their opponent's best state - a small, per-state baseline share (jittered slightly so it doesn't read as a suspiciously round number) now always survives, the same way Alf Landon still won a sliver of South Carolina's vote against FDR's 1936 landslide, or Trump still cleared a few points in DC in 2016. Raised the per-state cap on the third party's own share from 85% to 97% now that this floor is what actually prevents a major party from vanishing, rather than relying on the old cap to keep it away from that edge case.
  • Fixed the debug panel's "True national popular vote" row, which was showing the pre-third-party input margin rather than the actual realized one - when the third party is on and its siphon lean isn't perfectly symmetric, it genuinely shifts the national result away from that input (an asymmetric spoiler effect changes the outcome; that's the whole point of the setting), so a debug panel meant to expose the hidden truth should show the number the synthesized votes actually come out to, not the dial it started from. The "current poll says... off by" comparison right below it now measures against that same real number too.
  • Added a "Third party strength mode" toggle - "Random" (default, unchanged) draws the third party's national baseline strength fresh every run before the strength multiplier scales it, so the same multiplier setting can still land anywhere across a wide range cycle to cycle; "Exact" instead lets you type the national vote share directly (e.g. 8%) for a fully reproducible, fully controllable scenario, no randomness or formula involved. In "Exact" mode the strength multiplier's job narrows to just the home-state bump, since it no longer needs to also account for a hidden random draw.
  • Now that a third-party candidate can genuinely win outright (electoral college or the national popular vote), fixed several places that assumed the winner could only ever be D or R: the national popular vote's "final leader" check (used to decide whether a call needs correcting) could never resolve to the third party no matter how many votes they actually finished with, which could produce a bogus "CORRECTION" banner re-labeling a genuine third-party win as belonging to whichever major party had more votes between themselves - styled with that party's colors and portrait, even though both of them lost. The same "only D or R" assumption was fixed across the election-night call log, checkpoint popups, and album export: an outright electoral-college win for the third party now gets its own gold-styled victory banner and "clinches the presidency" call-log text (instead of being misreported as a contested/no-majority race or, worse, attributed to the Republican), and the final-tally recap's tipping-point race is ranked by the third party's own vote share when they're the winner, since the usual D-vs-R margin is meaningless once they've won.
  • The campaign forecast panel now shows a "Third-party win" probability tile (from the same Monte Carlo forecast that already computes it, previously computed but never displayed) whenever the third-party mechanic is on, and the live election-night win-probability line, race-overview title card, and persistent scoreboard all now show a real computed third-party win percentage instead of a permanently pinned "0.0% to win" - since a third-party candidate's electoral total is fixed the moment their strongest states are decided (nothing can flip a state to them mid-count in this model), that percentage is exact rather than simulated: 100% once their locked-in states alone clear a majority, 0% otherwise.
  • Fixed a bug from the third-party strength mode work that broke starting any campaign with the third-party mechanic turned off - the debug panel's "Third party" row was unconditionally reading the strength setting even when the mechanic was off (where it's null by design), throwing an error partway through rendering that silently stalled "Start campaign" with no on-screen indication of what went wrong.
  • The campaign forecast panel's "National poll" figure now reads directly off the national poll's own sampled D/R/(third-party)/Undecided shares (previously computed every step but discarded) instead of always reporting a D-vs-R margin, so it can genuinely show the third-party candidate leading the popular vote (e.g. "Perot+8.2") instead of misreporting whichever major party happens to have more votes between themselves.
  • Fixed the state polling table's default "closest first" sort, which ranked every state by the size of its D-vs-R margin even when a third-party candidate was the real story in that state - a state polling D 5% / R 4% / O 91% was sorting as a razor-thin D+1 toss-up near the top instead of the 86-point third-party blowout it actually is. The sort, and the small margin figure shown next to each state's share breakdown, now both measure the real leader's lead over the real runner-up. The same leader-vs-runner-up fix was applied to two tooltip rows that had been missed when the main "Poll" row was fixed for this earlier this cycle: the campaign map's debug-only "TRUE margin" row, and the election-night returns tooltip's "Final poll" and "TRUE final" rows.
  • Added a debug-panel row (third party only) spelling out the full national D/R/third-party/Undecided percentage split for both the true result and the current poll, and the existing "True national popular vote"/"Current poll says" rows now lead with the real leader's margin (e.g. "O+8.23 (D+1.34 two-party)") instead of only the two-party number, which couldn't say anything once a third-party candidate was genuinely ahead.
  • Poll percentages throughout sim2028 (tooltips, the state polling table's share breakdown, and the forecast panel's win-probability tiles) now show one decimal place instead of rounding to whole points.
  • Added a live numeric readout next to the third-party "Siphon lean" slider, since the slider alone made it hard to tell whether it was sitting at the default 0.5 or had drifted.
  • Fixed election night declaring "no candidate reaches the electoral votes needed... decided by the House of Representatives" while a third-party candidate could still genuinely win the presidency outright - the no-majority check only verified that the two major parties were each mathematically dead (their current total plus every remaining uncalled EV still fell short), never checking whether the third party's own best case fell short too, so it could jump straight to "House decides" while dozens of uncalled electoral votes still belonged to a third-party candidate who went on to clinch a majority hours later, producing an unnecessary and jarring "Correction... now projected to win the presidency instead" once those states were finally called.
  • Fixed the opening title card's "EST. NPV" stat, which always showed the D-vs-R two-party polling gap even when the campaign's own forecast (bridged from the same Election-Eve poll) had a third-party candidate genuinely leading the popular vote - it now reads off that poll's full D/R/third-party shares and names the real leader (e.g. "O+8.2"), matching the fix already made to the campaign forecast panel's own "National poll" figure.

Sim2028


  • The Electoral-votes-by-rating pill strip now hides the Tilt/Lean/Likely/Safe O tiers unless a third-party candidate is actually in the race - they were cluttering the legend with permanently-zero pills in the default two-party setup.
  • Added Tilt/Lean/Likely/Safe O rating tiers for a third-party candidate who's actually leading a state's polls, classified the same way as D/R (by the leader's margin over whoever's in second), and colored gold on the campaign map. The electoral-vote-by-rating bar and pill strip now count a real "O" total instead of silently folding a third-party lead into whichever of D/R happened to be bigger, and the pre-election "Tilt/Lean/Likely/Safe" badge shown during a live sim2028 election-night run (and saved into its album) is third-party-aware the same way.
  • Fixed the rating/map-color classification to read a state's own D/R/T poll shares directly rather than reconstructing them from a D-vs-R-only smoothed margin - a state polling 38% D / 37% T / 10% R was misreading as a safe D lock (the margin measures D's edge over R specifically, which stays huge even when a third-party candidate is actually running neck-and-neck) instead of the toss-up it really is. The campaign map's continuous fill now factors in the strongest rival too, so a state that's only "safe" against R but genuinely contested by a surging third party fades toward toss-up gray instead of staying deep blue.
  • The sim2028 forecast panel's "Exact tie" outcome is now "No majority," and actually modeled: with a third party able to win real states outright, nobody reaching 270 electoral votes is a real possibility (the scenario a contingent election exists for), not just an astronomically rare literal tie. The Monte Carlo now simulates each state's third-party share with its own uncertainty and gives a unit's electoral votes to whichever of D/R/O actually wins it.
  • Fixed election night's live margin badges (call/correction popups, lead-flip notices, final results, the national popular vote ticker) to measure a leader's margin against the actual runner-up instead of always against the other major party - a state where a third-party candidate is running a strong second was showing a badge like "R+29" (R's lead over a distant D) instead of the real "R+14" (R's lead over the third-party candidate actually in second place). The raw vote counts and "X ahead" figures were already correct; only the percentage-margin badge was wrong.
August 24, 2026

Sim2028


  • Rebuilt how monthly polls are generated. Each poll used to be a scalar D-R margin nudged by noise; it's now a genuine simulated poll - a state's (and separately, the nation's) hidden truth is expressed as a D/R/(third-party) vote-share point, an Undecided share is mixed in and shrinks across the campaign toward a small nonzero Election-Eve floor (nobody's poll is ever perfectly clean, even on the eve of the election), and 1,000 respondents are actually sampled from the resulting simplex every step. The one-time "mirage bias" that can make a state read the wrong way early in the campaign is still there, regionally correlated the same way it always was - it's the sampling noise on top that's new, replacing a flat statistical wobble with an actual poll of simulated people.
  • The state polling table and the map hover tooltip now show the real D/R/(Third party)/Undecided percentage breakdown a poll like that would report (e.g. "47% D · 44% R · 9% U"), with the projected margin kept underneath as a smaller reference line. Previously both only ever showed the margin.
  • This is purely a poll-generation change - the forecast model, the electoral snake, and election night's own vote synthesis are untouched and still consume the same relative-margin/national-popular-vote numbers as before.
  • Added Tilt/Lean/Likely/Safe O rating tiers for a third-party candidate who's actually leading a state's polls, classified the same way as D/R (by the leader's margin over whoever's in second), and colored gold on the campaign map. The electoral-vote-by-rating bar and pill strip now count a real "O" total instead of silently folding a third-party lead into whichever of D/R happened to be bigger, and the pre-election "Tilt/Lean/Likely/Safe" badge shown during a live sim2028 election-night run (and saved into its album) is third-party-aware the same way.

Election Night


  • Album captions (the text under each image in the "Election Night Album" grid) now render in full caps, and no longer say a state was "called" - a normal call now reads "STATE PROJECTED FOR CANDIDATE," reserving "CANDIDATE WINS STATE" for the rare case where the remaining uncounted votes in that state can no longer mathematically overcome the leader's margin. A lead-change caption now reads "CANDIDATE TAKES THE LEAD IN STATE."
  • Replaced the hardcoded list of seven states (WI/MI/PA/AZ/GA/NV/NC) that were always treated as 2028 "key races" regardless of how competitive a given simulated run actually made them. A state now counts as a key race if its pre-election rating is Toss-up or Tilt D/R, or if its true simulated margin is itself within a Toss-up-sized margin of even - so a landslide run no longer force-highlights states that weren't actually close.
  • The "takes the lead" checkpoint card (live sim and album) now shows a full D/R vote-total comparison box - raw counts, share of total, and how many votes the new leader is ahead by - matching what the state call card already shows. A third-party row now also appears on it under the same conditions it would appear on a call screen (a real 2028 sim run with third party enabled, or a historical year with a genuine third-party showing like 1968/1992/1996).

Sim2028


  • Fixed the third-party candidate feature (added yesterday, still beta) so its numbers actually reach the screen. The debug panel's "True result" line was computed by code written back when the simulator was strictly two-party, so it silently folded any third-party win into D or R instead of showing it - it's now a single, unified three-way tally that always agrees with the rest of the debug panel.
  • The map's popular-vote badges had a hardcoded "Other" row that never displayed, even with a real third-party vote share flowing through - it now appears (with its own vote total and percentage) whenever the third party is drawing any votes, and stays hidden otherwise.
  • A state the third party actually won could render blue instead of gold, because a cached color from a previous render was being trusted ahead of the freshly-computed result. The real winner's color is no longer masked by a stale cache.
  • The post-election "album" summary had its own separate, two-party-only winner check that would mislabel a third-party win as a Republican win. It now recognizes a third-party winner the same way the rest of the app already does.
  • Added a "(beta)" note to the Third party setting, since the mechanic itself (not just this round's display bugs) may still need more tuning.
  • The state-call popup's third-party comparison row was still gated behind a 5% floor meant for historical noise-filtering (a real Libertarian/Green showing shouldn't clutter every call) - but for a real sim2028 run, any showing the setting produces is worth seeing, so that floor no longer applies when third party is actually running in this simulator (historical replays keep the original 5% floor).

Election Night


  • Fixed a bug in the new sim2028 pre-election rating badge (see yesterday's entry): at-large units (ME-AL/NE-AL) always showed "Toss-up" regardless of their actual pre-election rating, because they never get a poll prior of their own - it now derives their rating from the same vote-weighted composite of their districts' priors that already feeds the "key race" flag, instead of silently reading a missing margin as 0.

Sim2028


  • Added an "Electoral snake" chart to the campaign step: a FiveThirtyEight-style ribbon of every state/district ordered from safest-Republican to safest-Democratic, sized by electoral votes and colored on the same red-blue ramp as the map, wrapped row by row like a snake so it doesn't run one very wide strip. A dashed line marks the 270th electoral vote and calls out the tipping-point state.
  • A state whose ribbon segment straddles a row break (e.g. a big state landing right at a row's edge) is shown as one continuous piece - joined by a same-color, same-thickness corner block, labeled once, and highlighted together on hover - rather than two disconnected fragments.
  • Tiny 1-2 EV units (the ME/NE congressional districts, the ME-AL/NE-AL at-large pairs) get a little extra rendering width so their abbreviation actually fits; this only affects layout, not the real EV total or where the 270 line falls.
  • Clicking a segment glows the matching state on the map, reusing the same highlight the polling-trendline legend already has. The chart sits directly under the map (above Forecast/Trendlines/Table) so that click doesn't require scrolling back up.
  • Added an optional third-party/independent candidate, off by default. Picked the same way as the two major-party candidates (custom name or historical search, no presets), with a home state and hometown strength. Its strength is heaviest in states that are already safe for one side - real protest-vote behavior - and suppressed in genuine swing states, so it dampens landslides and can occasionally spoil or win a state on election night without routinely blocking anyone from reaching 270. A new "siphon lean" slider sets which major party it draws more votes from (or split evenly by default). Only affects the final election-night result - the campaign's polls and forecast stay two-party throughout.
  • Added a "Region bleed" setting (independent of the third party toggle) that extends every candidate's home-state advantage, at reduced strength, into the rest of their home region and into neighboring regions, instead of stopping at their exact home state.
  • Added a "Third party strength" control: the initial calibration turned out too weak to ever plausibly win a state, even with a maxed-out home-state bonus. It's now a multiplier on the whole mechanic (national showing and home-state bump alike) that can be pushed well above the 1.0x default to make outright wins and landslide-state captures achievable. Also removed a floor that had been protecting the major parties from ever being fully squeezed out of a state.
  • The debug panel (?debug=true) now always shows a "Third party" section - candidate, strength, and siphon lean when enabled (or "off" when not), electoral votes actually won, and the states where the third party did best with how close each one came to flipping - so it's no longer ambiguous whether the mechanic is doing anything when a run doesn't happen to produce a dramatic result.

Election Night


  • Fixed a bug where the "X is projected to be elected president" breaking-news slide could appear in a checkpoint before the tally shown alongside it had actually crossed the electoral-vote threshold. The checkpoint's breaking-news-first sort pulled the projection to the front of the batch along with the other marquee calls, ahead of the ordinary (non-key-race) state calls that were mathematically necessary to reach the threshold but got stuck in the routine back tier. The projection no longer jumps the queue - it's sorted by its own timestamp like a routine call, which naturally lands it right after the call that actually tipped the count over.

The same badge appears (in a terser bare-label form) on the "Races to watch" cards and the final "Key Races" recap, in the space between the reporting bar and the margin badge.
  • The badge only appears for actual sim2028 forecast runs, never for historical-year replays, since a historical replay's "prior" is a synthesized stand-in rather than a real pre-election fact.
  • Shown in both the live simulator and the downloadable Election Night Album.

Election Night


  • The very first checkpoint of election night (the KY/IN poll-close batch) now opens with a "portrait of the race" title card - the election name, each major-party candidate's full name and photo in a party-colored border, and a smaller photo for the major third-party candidate too in years with a real electoral-vote haul for one (e.g. 1968, 1992, 1996). Shown in both the live simulator and the Election Night Album.
  • In the sim2028 simulator specifically, that opening title card also shows a small stats row: projected win probability for each candidate (capped at 99.9% either way), the probability of an electoral college tie, an estimated national popular vote margin from the starting polls, and a median electoral vote estimate.
  • Each candidate's photo border hugs the actual portrait instead of a generic square, so a non-square photo doesn't end up with empty letterboxed corners inside the border.
  • The opening title card's win %/tie %/median-EV/NPV stats (sim2028 only) now come from the campaign's own Election-Eve forecast - the same estimate shown on the sim2028 forecast panel - instead of the live election-night win-probability model, which is deliberately hedged close to 50-50 in the earliest minutes of the count since it hasn't seen any actual returns yet.
  • The default election-night playback speed is now 1.0x instead of 0.5x, on both the historical simulator and sim2028.
  • The final "Key Races" recap no longer ends on a sparse last page (e.g. just one or two cards): it now fills any partial final page out to a full 6 with the next-closest remaining races by margin, so a checkpoint that would've spanned 3 pages shows 18 races instead of trailing off after 13.
  • The mid-night "Races to watch" slide can now span up to 2 pages (12 races) instead of always capping at 6, so a busy stretch of the night with many uncalled/key races isn't truncated - it still shows just 1 page when there aren't enough races to fill a second.
  • The national popular vote is now pinned first on that "Races to watch" slide (badged KEY) once any votes have been counted, alongside the state races - previously it only showed up as a standalone card in the always-visible call-log sidebar, not in the checkpoint popup/album at all.
  • The national popular vote can now get its own "takes the lead" breaking-news card when its raw count changes hands while still uncalled, same as a key state race - and, if it's ever called and then genuinely narrows back into too-close-to-call territory (rare, but possible on a razor-thin national margin), a "too close to call" retraction card followed by a normal re-call once it's decided again. Shown in both the live simulator and the Election Night Album.

Aleck Lickman (v0.1)


  • Added a first pass at pundit commentary for election night: Aleck Lickman, a parody of Allan Lichtman, predicts the winner using his "13 Beets to the Presidency" system, then reacts once the final result is in.
  • Right after the opening title card, Lickman announces how many of his 13 beets came back false and confidently calls a winner by name. For any real election (every year the 13 Keys page covers), he uses the actual graded false-key count directly, so his historical track record matches the real Lichtman system's exactly - including its famous hits (2000, 2016, 2020) and its 2024 miss. Only the fictional 2028 race has no real keys to grade, so there he estimates the false-beet count from the true national popular vote instead, via the same least-squares fit the 13 Keys page uses (false keys vs. incumbent-relative NPV margin), inverted and run through a seeded margin of error - so he can be confidently wrong there too, same as the real Keys.
  • After the final tally (and any key-race recap), Lickman gives a closing reaction - one of four flavors depending on whether his prediction matched the popular-vote winner, the electoral-college winner, both, or neither.
  • Shown in both the live simulator and the (headless) Election Night Album. Optional and on by default via a new "Show Aleck Lickman commentary" toggle next to "Show state-call updates," on the historical simulator and sim2028 alike.
  • The 2028 estimate's wobble is seeded off whatever's actually driving that run's randomness (sim2028's own campaign seed, or the historical page's random-PV seed, or just the year when nothing else is randomized), so replaying the same setup calls the same beet count instead of re-rolling on every Start/Reset.
  • Mid-night reactive commentary (as calls move toward/away from his prediction) isn't wired up yet, but the dialogue for it has been drafted for later.
  • ?debug=true on the sim2028 page now also surfaces Lickman's false-beet count, and (for the 2028 estimate specifically) the underlying regression fit.

sim2028 candidates


  • A searched (historical) candidate pick can now be given a home state and hometown strength, same as a custom candidate - the sim doesn't try to guess or impose one, you set it yourself.
  • A preset 2028 candidate's hometown strength (previously always fixed at full) is now adjustable too, via the same slider - their home state is still fixed (e.g. Ossoff/GA, Vance/OH), but now shown next to the strength slider (disabled, since it can't be changed) instead of hidden.

Election Night: Album


  • The Election Night Album now generates its own, much tighter checkpoint schedule instead of reusing the live sim's - the live popup is tuned to minimize interruptions, but the album is a static image set with nobody to interrupt, so it now paces out into noticeably more, smaller "photos" across the night
  • Fixed poll-close cards sometimes displaying out of chronological order - a "Polls just closed" card could previously get pulled behind a later breaking call within the same batch (e.g. Alaska/Hawaii's 1am close showing up after a Pennsylvania call that happened 18 minutes later); poll-close now always forces a clean chapter break at its true moment
  • Breaking call/correction/retraction/lead-change cards now show the "Breaking news" ribbon at the top of the image, matching the live popup - previously only the outcome/final-tally cards got the ribbon
  • "Races to watch" cards are now paced independently instead of tagging along with every checkpoint: they show up during a genuine lull in the action (so a tense race's margin narrowing between calls is actually visible) and right before a chapter break like a poll-close, rather than after nearly every single call
  • Fixed the map snapshot right after a "Polls just closed" card looking identical to the one before it (nothing new has counted yet at the exact instant polls close) - it now waits until partway to the next checkpoint before capturing, so there's actually something new to see, without ever showing a call before its own card has announced it
  • Fixed map snapshots barely changing between consecutive images - with the album's tighter checkpoint schedule, a map was still being captured at every single checkpoint (some only minutes apart), which just produced near-duplicates; map snapshots now pace independently the same way "Races to watch" cards do, capturing during a real lull or at a chapter boundary rather than at every checkpoint
  • "Races to watch" cards now show a timestamp, both on the image and in its caption, like every other card kind already did
  • Fixed a double-counting bug in the running EV tally: when a breaking lead-change card got sorted ahead of calls it chronologically followed within the same batch (e.g. a key race "takes the lead" card shown before that batch's California/Oregon calls), the tally shown on it had already absorbed those later-displayed calls' electoral votes - which then got added a second time as their own cards followed, visibly inflating the count

Bellwethers

  • Added a new display mode: "Outcome Table (Streaks)".
  • Rather than looking at states/units which are barometric bellwethers (states close to the national popular vote), this table shows states/units that have had long streaks of voting for the winning presidential candidate.
  • Users can set a streak threshold (default 5 elections) and choose to allow up to one or two misses in the streak.
  • The table lists states/units that meet the criteria, showing their longest qualifying streaks, as well as the most recent streak.
  • Allow users to view popular vote winner streaks or electoral vote winner streaks.
  • Added a reverse streaks option to show states that have consistently voted against the winning candidate.

Laplace

  • Added a little naive Monte Carlo simulation tool to estimate probabilities based on Laplace smoothing.
  • Users can set the number of simulations to run.
  • The results table shows the likelihood of each state to be a tipping point based on a simplified Normal model using Laplace probabilities.
  • Clicking on these states brings up a detailed view with more information: showing the differences between a scenario's map at the tipping point and the 2024 map, as well as a basic ranking of states by their relative margin in the scenario.
  • Also included is a table showing the percent of simulations a state was a bellwether within 5 percentage points of the national popular vote and the percentage of simulations it was more democratic than the national popular vote.

  • Added a Laplace method to generate future endpoints (set as default)
  • This uses a Bayesian approach similar to the Laplace page to give a more probabilistic estimate of future margins (allowing for uncertainty). Ex. CA trended blue significantly until 2020, but has started to trend red. But the line of best fit still has a firmly positive slope. Thus, the old method would almost certainly predict a continued blue trend, unless you used a different slope method. Whereas the Laplace method gives about a 50:50 chance of CA trending either direction, by weighting by recency and giving some uncertainty.
