Changelog

A record of changes, updates, and improvements to this site.

August 18, 2026

Election Night


  • A dramatic full-screen "Breaking News" flash now plays for a beat before a key-race call, any correction or retraction, the call that actually clinches/loses a majority, or the final-tally/no-longer-decided moments - like a broadcast cutting to a big-story sting before revealing the result
  • Within a single state-call popup batch, these "breaking" slides are now grouped and shown first ("we can now call Georgia for Biden - also, Trump wins North Dakota and Wyoming"), so the flash above only has to play once per batch instead of once per key race; the always-final "Final tally" screen still always shows last
  • The "Races to watch" list - both the checkpoint popup's slide and the always-on sidebar - now pins key races (states predicted close pre-election, or genuinely close in the true result even when the pre-election signal missed it) to the top, each marked with a small "KEY" tag, ahead of the existing live-drama-based ordering
  • Fixed a bug where a state predicted comfortable but that actually ended up razor-thin (e.g. New Mexico in 2000, Michigan in 2016) could be skipped for "key race" emphasis entirely, since the emphasis was based only on the (occasionally noisy) pre-election prediction; it's now based on whichever of the prediction or the true result was closer
  • Fixed a bug where the specific call that pushed a candidate over the top (or knocked them back below a majority) could itself display as a routine, unmarked call even though the very next screen announced them winning the presidency, with the electoral vote scoreboard not yet reflecting the majority - that deciding call is now always treated as breaking news itself, and always grouped immediately before the win announcement it caused
  • Any correction or retraction is now always treated as breaking news (previously only when the state itself was independently a "key race")
  • Removed the separate "Final" flash text for the night's final-tally screen - it now just says "Breaking News" like every other flash, instead of a distinct label that read oddly on its own

Election Night: Retraction Drama & Lead-Change Alerts


  • Toned down how big a wrongly-leaning state's initial called margin could get (e.g. Florida 2000 called for Gore) - it had drifted up to an implausible ~D+11; it's back to the believable ~D+4-5 range it had before
  • Added drama to the "too close to call" period after a state gets retracted: most of the time now, once a state is un-called, the count swings toward its TRUE eventual winner with a fresh, sizeable lead that agonizingly narrows back down to the real final margin as counting finishes (the "Bush surges to +10,000, then it shrinks to 537" effect) - the old slower "creep from wrong to right" still shows up occasionally for variety
  • Added "Breaking News" alerts for key races when the raw vote lead changes hands while a race is still uncalled - before its first call, or during too-close-to-call limbo after a retraction - mirroring a broadcast flashing a lead change (e.g. "takes the lead in Georgia"); these can pop up as their own standalone checkpoint during a quiet stretch, but don't clutter the persistent call log
August 17, 2026

Election Night


  • Key swing-state calls now get visual emphasis: calls for states predicted to be competitive going in (based on pre-election polls for 2028, or synthesized plausible polls for historical years) are marked with a "Breaking news" ribbon and an accent-colored border glow, and display on screen 20% longer than routine calls, so viewers know when Florida or Arizona is being called vs. Wyoming
  • Key calls are also highlighted in the always-on "Call log" sidebar with bolder text and a thicker accent border
  • The playback footer (Pause/Resume, Reset, speed, progress) now stays visible and clickable during a state-call popup instead of being covered by it
  • Pause during a state call now freezes that call on its current slide (auto-advance stops) while still letting you click, tap, or press space/enter/arrow-left to manually step through the remaining slides; the sim clock stays paused afterward until you press Resume
  • Reset now works mid-state-call, immediately closing the popup and exiting election night mode, same as it already did between calls
  • "Elected"/"Reelected" wording is now reserved for the night's true final tally; every earlier clinch or corrected-call moment reads as "Projected to be elected/reelected" instead, since the winner isn't settled yet at that point
  • Fixed the state-call popup's electoral vote scoreboard, which could show incorrect totals in a few cases: two states called at the same moment (e.g. 2000's Georgia and South Carolina), a state's later correction, and split-elector states like 1960 Alabama/Mississippi; the whole night's tally is now precomputed up front instead of recalculated live, so it's no longer sensitive to playback speed
  • The scoreboard's "+N"/"-N" delta chip now appears for every call, not just corrections, so you can see at a glance how many electoral votes a state just added
  • Delta chips now fade out smoothly instead of disappearing abruptly, and read more clearly against their background
  • Fixed a bug where, if the last state call/correction of the night locked in an Electoral College tie (or otherwise knocked the leader below a majority) at the exact same moment as the night's true final tally, the popup could get stuck on a "No longer decided" breaking-news screen and never reach the real final "Decided by the House of Representatives" screen
  • A called state can now be retracted ("too close to call") mid-count if its confidence collapses well before it finishes reporting, then called again later once it recovers - not just corrected at the very end, matching how Florida was called for Gore in 2000 and then explicitly un-called. Retracted calls stay in the call log alongside the eventual re-call, the map/tooltip/small-box coloring reverts to in-progress while a state is retracted, and the state-call scoreboard/checkpoint popups account for it so electoral votes are never double-counted
  • States whose true winner matches the eventual Electoral College winner are now somewhat more likely to show a fake early lean toward the eventual loser before correcting, and states whose true winner is the eventual loser are somewhat less likely to falsely lean toward the winner - the same "wrong candidate leads early" drama, just tilted to happen more often on the side of the eventual loser rather than padding the eventual winner's early lead
  • A retracted call now gets its own popup screen (previously it fell through to a broken "WINNER" card with a placeholder avatar) - a distinct amber-red "too close to call" screen showing who was previously called
  • Fake early leads now swing bigger for states already fake-leaning toward the wrong candidate, and a retracted state now stays "too close to call" for at least 30 simulated minutes before it can be called again, instead of potentially flipping back right away

2028 Campaign Simulator


  • Added a "Candidates" picker to the Setup panel: pick a preset D or R candidate (Beshear, Buttigieg, Ocasio-Cortez, Newsom, Ossoff for D; Rubio, Vance for R), or go "Custom…" to type any name and optionally upload your own portrait
  • A custom portrait can now be dragged and dropped anywhere onto a candidate's box, not just picked via the file button; the native file input's own "No file chosen" label is a browser security limit and will never reflect drag-dropped or reloaded images, but a live thumbnail preview next to it shows the actual state, plus a "Remove image" button to clear it
  • Fixed an uploaded custom portrait not surviving a page reload
  • Candidate picks are display-only (call-log popups, map tooltips) and have no effect on the simulated result, matching how the model has always worked
  • Preset and custom-name picks are saved to this browser and also round-trip through "Share URL"; an uploaded custom portrait is saved to this browser only
August 16, 2026

Election Night


  • Fixed a national-popular-vote accounting bug in election night for pre-district-split Maine/Nebraska years (for example 1880): ME-AL/NE-AL rows are now treated as normal statewide vote contributors before the split years, so the end-of-night NPV margin/count no longer drifts to an incorrect D lead.
  • Added a checkpoint popup that periodically interrupts the night to recap what's happened: new calls, corrections, and a running scoreboard, batched together instead of firing one at a time for every single state
  • The scoreboard box in the popup shows a live electoral vote tally for each party with an animated count-up, plus each party's live win probability
  • Added a genuine tie/no-majority probability alongside the D and R win percentages ("D 48.2% — R 46.5% — Tie 5.3%"), so a contingent-election scenario can actually be represented instead of forcing a binary D-or-R read. The tie percentage now reads an honest 0.0% once a no-majority result becomes mathematically provable, rather than being stuck at a token nonzero floor
  • The election is now called as a no-majority/contingent election as soon as it becomes mathematically impossible for either party to reach 270, instead of waiting for every last state to be called
  • Years with a serious third-party electoral vote haul (1968, 1948, 1912, 1860, etc.) now show that party in the persistent running EV tally, not just D and R
  • Third-party leads now show a real computed margin ("O+13.1", with the matching raw vote count underneath) everywhere a D/R margin would normally appear — call log, races-to-watch, and checkpoint slides — instead of a generic "Other lead" placeholder
  • Added a "races to watch" slide highlighting the closest uncalled contests, and a dedicated slide for the final result once the night wraps up
  • Uncropped candidate portraits on the "X ELECTED PRESIDENT" slide so the full portrait shows instead of a cropped circle
  • Lowered the default call-confidence threshold from 0.5 to 0.15, making calls come in earlier by default
  • Fixed the night sometimes ending before a razor-thin national popular vote race (e.g. 1880) ever got officially called
  • Fixed the 1948 "flip to no-majority" scenario not actually working, caused by an Alabama data quirk (Thurmond electors recorded under the Democratic line) that was letting Truman keep votes he shouldn't have had
  • Fixed checkpoint scheduling so results don't jump around or rewind, and so the final recap slide reliably shows up at the end of the night instead of occasionally getting stranded
  • Fixed the sim2028 election-night button swapping in the election-night footer immediately on click, which broke navigating back through the campaign timeline before pressing Start; the footer now only switches once election night actually begins, and switches back correctly on Reset
  • Winner announcements (checkpoint popup and call log) now say which numbered presidency is on the line — "Elected 47th President" for a new president, or "Reelected as 47th President" when an incumbent (or a former president winning a non-consecutive term, like Cleveland 1892, T. Roosevelt 1912, or Trump 2024) wins another term. Applies to the main election-night and sim2028 simulations only; future.html's hypothetical years are unaffected
  • Fixed a same-term incumbent's reelection ordinal being off by one (e.g. Coolidge showing as "reelected as 31st president" instead of the 30th president he actually was) — a reelected incumbent now correctly keeps their existing number instead of the next one
  • Clarified the attributions page to explicitly state that all images on the site (including candidate portraits) are public-domain images sourced from Wikimedia Commons
  • Added missing candidate portraits for Ralph Nader (2000) and James B. Weaver (1880 Populist candidate)
August 15, 2026

Election Night


  • Added a live national win-probability estimate that updates throughout the night as states report, based on a statistical model of each state's remaining uncertainty
  • The estimate accounts for a shared national mood, not just each state's own results in isolation: as states report, it infers a shared national swing and regional swings (New England, Rust Belt, South, etc.) from how those states are over- or under-performing their pre-election expectations
  • Tuned the pacing of the estimate to feel like an actual election night rather than a spreadsheet: it starts more uncertain and narrows gradually as real results come in, instead of jumping to near-certainty the moment a handful of states report
  • Reworked the call log into a dedicated, full-height sidebar on wide screens, colorized by leader, showing the national popular vote and each call's raw vote margin alongside the standard percentage
  • Added a downloadable call-log export (TXT/CSV) covering the full night, including a timeline of the model's national and regional swing estimates
  • The displayed confidence percentage now scales against whatever call threshold you've set, instead of always assuming the default — set a stricter or looser threshold and the "how sure are we" reading adjusts to match
  • The declared national outcome ("X clinches the presidency") now only reflects results that have actually been made public; if a correction to a state's call changes who's actually projected to win, that gets its own explicit log entry instead of the banner silently changing
  • Fixed vote counting on very close races: it used to stall near 100% for a stretch and then jump straight to the final tally once a state finished reporting; counting now proceeds smoothly all the way through
  • Fixed the reporting percentage showing "100.0% counted" while ballots were technically still left to count; it now only shows 100% once every ballot is actually in
  • The map's hover tooltips now show the same confidence and reporting figures as the call log, instead of a rougher, less consistent version
  • Added a dedicated playback footer (play/pause, reset, speed, and the progress scrubber) that appears at the bottom of the screen during election night mode, so the controls stay reachable without scrolling back up
  • Smaller touches: candidate last names in call announcements, a year label in the call log header, free-text speed input instead of a dropdown, and a corrected confidence readout for exact ties
  • Added real candidate portraits for every presidential and third-party candidate back through the 1800s, replacing placeholder art across the election-night map, call log, and checkpoint slides
  • Standardized portrait filenames to a consistent [YEAR][Lastname] scheme and refreshed several low-quality or mismatched historical images

July 17, 2026

Trending States


  • Added a "swing state ETA" to the Trending States page: a naive straight-line extrapolation of how many years, at a state's current competitiveness trend, until it crosses into (or out of) the swing-state band — projected to the nearest presidential election year for readability
  • Added two new categories driven by that ETA rather than raw trend speed: "Upcoming Swing States" (not competitive yet, but on track to get there soonest) and "Fading Battlegrounds" (competitive today, but trending toward becoming safe for one party soonest)
  • Added a third trend-map mode, "Swing ETA," that lights up states by how soon they're projected to become (or stop being) a swing state
  • Fixed the scatter plot being dominated by a couple of outlier states, which crushed the rest into an unreadable cluster — switched to a symlog scale so states near the middle get more room
  • Fixed the competitiveness trend map rendering as solid gray due to a unit-conversion bug in the color thresholds
  • Added "2028 Mode": a slider to set a hypothetical national popular-vote margin, holding each state's current lean relative to the national environment fixed (optionally also carrying its trend forward to 2028), plus a "Projected Battlegrounds" list and a fourth trend-map mode showing which states would actually be close under that scenario
  • Distinguished this "close under a scenario" concept from the existing "Swing"/"Bellwether" language (which measures a state's margin relative to the national environment, not its absolute closeness) in the methodology notes, to match the terminology already used on the Bellwether Explorer page
  • 2028 Mode now also projects an electoral vote tally (D vs. R) for the chosen scenario, using each unit's real electoral vote weight — including Maine's and Nebraska's split-EV districts — so the projection reproduces the real electoral college structure automatically
  • The 2028 Mode panel is now hidden until "2028 Projection" is selected on the trend map, instead of always showing
  • "Fading Battlegrounds" now covers all of today's swing states (not just the ones already trending away), ranked by how soon they'd stop being competitive if their trend continues; states still trending more competitive show that trend instead of a meaningless ETA
  • Fixed the competitiveness trend calculation: it previously fit a separate regression to |relative margin|, which breaks down for any state whose lean crosses zero within the trend window (e.g. NE-02, R-leaning as recently as 2016, now D-leaning) — it could badly misread a state that's actually solidifying as "narrowing." Replaced with sign(current lean) × direction trend, the correct derivative of |relative margin|, which is robust to that case
  • "2028 Mode" now defaults to including each state's current trend, and its checkbox default reflects the actual page state instead of being hardcoded
  • Every tooltip now shows a live "2028 estimate" reflecting the current 2028 Mode scenario settings
  • Reworded the Emerging Battlegrounds / Solidifying States value labels from "pts/decade narrower/wider" to "pts/decade toward/away from solid D or R," naming which party's territory the trend points toward
  • Fixed ME-AL and NE-AL having no way to be seen or interacted with on the trend map — the congressional-district overlay covers their entire visible area, so their statewide value was neither visible nor clickable. Wired up the map's existing (until now unused) small-state-box overlay, which also gives Rhode Island, DC, and the other geographically tiny states their own hoverable/clickable chip, and added persistent state labels to the map to match the rest of the site
  • Unified "Fading Battlegrounds" around a single "next lean category" ETA (e.g. "~7 yrs → Tilt D") for every current swing state, not just the ones already re-widening — a state can be on track to leave swing status entirely, or just to keep drifting through it toward one side, and both now get a consistent, ranked estimate
  • Updated the methodology notes to match the corrected competitiveness formula and the new next-category ETA concept

PCA Explorer


  • Added a new page, pca.html, that runs principal component analysis on state presidential margins live in the browser (via numeric.js's SVD, the same dependency already used by the "Future" page's synthetic scenario generator) for whatever metric and year range you pick — no precomputed/fixed dataset
  • A unit is only included if it has a value for every year in the selected range (no imputation), which is why the default 1964–2024 range gives exactly 51 units (50 states + DC); a "Split ME/NE districts" toggle swaps Maine and Nebraska's statewide totals for their congressional-district results when the range fully supports it (Maine from 1972, Nebraska from 1992)
  • Visualizations: a scree plot, component scores over time, and a shared US map with three modes — component loadings, an interactive reconstruction driven by per-component sliders (with a live electoral vote tally), and a historical "how far off is a low-rank reconstruction" mode that flags which states a truncated set of components would have called for the other party
  • A varimax rotation toggle (ported from the existing offline pca/do_pca.py analysis) for more concentrated, easier-to-read loadings
  • A panel ranking every component by how well it correlates with the national popular vote, with least-squares fits in both directions
  • Added a link to the PCA Explorer in the site header

  • Added a new page, trending-states.html, that ranks all 50 states + DC (plus Maine's and Nebraska's congressional districts) by their recent political trajectory

  • The number of recent elections used for the trend window is adjustable (3–8 elections back, defaulting to the last 5)
  • Updated header to include a link to the Trending States page

Bellwethers

  • Added a new display mode: "Outcome Table (Streaks)".
  • Rather than looking at states/units which are barometric bellwethers (states close to the national popular vote), this table shows states/units that have had long streaks of voting for the winning presidential candidate.
  • Users can set a streak threshold (default 5 elections) and choose to allow up to one or two misses in the streak.
  • The table lists states/units that meet the criteria, showing their longest qualifying streaks, as well as the most recent streak.
  • Allow users to view popular vote winner streaks or electoral vote winner streaks.
  • Added a reverse streaks option to show states that have consistently voted against the winning candidate.

Laplace

  • Added a little naive Monte Carlo simulation tool to estimate probabilities based on Laplace smoothing.
  • Users can set the number of simulations to run.
  • The results table shows the likelihood of each state to be a tipping point based on a simplified Normal model using Laplace probabilities.
  • Clicking on these states brings up a detailed view with more information: showing the differences between a scenario's map at the tipping point and the 2024 map, as well as a basic ranking of states by their relative margin in the scenario.
  • Also included is a table showing the percent of simulations a state was a bellwether within 5 percentage points of the national popular vote and the percentage of simulations it was more democratic than the national popular vote.
  • There are still some bugs and rough edges to work out, but it's a start.

  • This uses a Bayesian approach similar to the Laplace page to give a more probabilistic estimate of future margins (allowing for uncertainty). Ex. CA trended blue significantly until 2020, but has started to trend red. But the line of best fit still has a firmly positive slope. Thus, the old method would almost certainly predict a continued blue trend, unless you used a different slope method.
