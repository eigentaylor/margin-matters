Methodology
  • Relative margin: a state's presidential margin (D − R) minus the national margin that year. Using relative margin instead of the raw margin filters out nationwide swings, so a state isn't flagged as "trending" just because a wave hit every state that year — it isolates that state's own trajectory.
  • Direction trend: the slope of relative margin over the selected window of recent elections (least-squares fit), shown in points per decade. Positive = trending Democratic, negative = trending Republican.
  • Competitiveness trend: the slope of the distance from even (|relative margin|) over the same window. A shrinking distance means the state is becoming more competitive ("Emerging Battlegrounds"); a growing distance means it's becoming safer for whichever party already leads there ("Solidifying States").
  • Maine's and Nebraska's congressional districts (which each award their own electoral vote) are tracked separately from their statewide results, since they're often targeted independently by campaigns.
  • A state can appear in two categories at once — e.g. a state can be trending Republican while also becoming more competitive, if it started out safely Democratic and is drifting toward purple.
  • Categories require at least 3 elections of data and only include states actually moving in that category's direction (so "Trending Democratic" never fills up with states that are actually trending Republican, just less severely).
  • Swing state ETA: a naive straight-line extrapolation of the competitiveness trend — if a state keeps narrowing (or widening) at exactly its current rate, how many years until it crosses the swing-state line (within 5 points of even, relative to the national environment)? Political trends don't actually stay linear for decades, so treat this as a rough "at this rate" estimate, not a real forecast. "Upcoming Swing States" and "Fading Battlegrounds" rank states by this ETA rather than by raw trend speed — a state can trend quickly but still be decades from actually becoming competitive if it's starting from a very safe margin.
