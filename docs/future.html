Formal Explanation: We project each state’s 2048 margin from its 2000–2024 trend (lightly shrunk), then draw a Brownian bridge (a random but endpoint-anchored wander) around the straight line from 2024 to 2048. The bridge’s volatility is higher early than late, and each step mixes shared national shocks (from PCA factors) with state-specific noise. We soft-bound extremes and keep Maine/Nebraska at-large equal to their vote-weighted district averages. The parameters are tuned so the simulated paths match historical features like typical step sizes, zig-zags, overshoots, and the timing of peak detours, without overfitting.

The key metric is relative margin. This is the difference between the state's raw margin and the national popular vote. This gives us a better sense of how left or right a state is by removing the national movement. For example, Georgia moved 2.4 points right from 2020 to 2024. But the nation moved 6 points right. Thus, relative to the nation, GA actually moved 3.6 points left. Indeed, relative to the nation, Georgia has moved left consistently every election since 2008. Thus, our model looks at these dynamics, rather than the year to year fluctuations to say that Georgia is consistently trending left. Will Georgia be D+7.8 in 2048? I have no idea. But based on the data, that is "plausible" enough for this silly little model.

We do not bother to estimate future census data, so we just use the electoral votes from 2024.
